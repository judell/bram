  Pedaling Through Time: A History of the Bicycle

  The bicycle occupies a peculiar place in the story of human invention. It is neither as ancient as the wheel nor
  as dramatically transformative as the steam engine, yet few machines have reshaped everyday life so quietly and so
  completely. From dusty German park paths in the early nineteenth century to congested megacities of the
  twenty-first, the bicycle has been a toy of aristocrats, a working-class necessity, a feminist instrument, a
  wartime courier, and an emblem of ecological hope. Its history is not merely a chronicle of gears and frames; it
  is a history of how people learned to move through the world under their own power, with elegance, risk, and
  stubborn persistence.
